## 1. Current codebase — what exists and what is reusable

### 2026-10-20 — Repository map & current pipeline
**Subagent question:** What existing code is relevant to multi-person keyword +
speaker identification, and what are its constraints?
**Answer:**
- `src/models/disent_v2.py` defines `DISENT_KWS_v2(embed_dim=192, temporal_ch=48)`.
  `forward(audio, p_spk=None, p_kw=None)` returns `(z_phn, z_spk)`; conditioning
  is built only when **both** prototypes are supplied
  (`cond = cat([p_spk, p_kw])`, lines 46–48). With both `None`, the model returns
  **unconditioned** 192-d embeddings (lines 50–52). This is the path used by
  `extract_prototypes`.
- `src/models/scorer.py` `DualGateScorer.forward(z_phn, z_spk, p_kw, p_spk)`
  normalizes all vectors and returns `(score, sim_kw, sim_spk)` where
  `score = softplus(w_kw)*sim_kw + softplus(w_spk)*sim_spk` (lines 39–47).
  Cosine broadcasting `(1,D)` vs `(P,D)` works and returns `(P,)` (verified in
  prior run and re-checked by reading lines 44–45).
- `src/enrollment/enroll.py`:
  - `LFBETransform` (lines 40–63): `torchaudio.transforms.MelSpectrogram(
    sample_rate=16000, n_fft=400, hop_length=160, n_mels=80, f_min=20, f_max=7600)`,
    then pads/truncates waveform to `MAX_AUDIO_SEC=2.0` (32,000 samples),
    `log(spec + 1e-6)`, returns `(80, 200)`.
  - `trim_silence` (lines 15–39): 1600-sample (100 ms) energy frames, threshold
    `max_db - 30`, pads 200 ms.
  - `augment_enrollment` (lines 86–116): pitch shifts ±1/±2 semitones, speed
    0.90–1.10, gain ±1.5/±3 dB; requires ≥5 waveforms; default 30 variants.
  - `extract_prototypes` (lines 119–140): for each waveform, `trim_silence` →
    `LFBETransform` → `model(feat)` **unconditioned** → mean over variants →
    `F.normalize`. Returns `p_kw`, `p_spk`, each `(1,192)`.
  - `enroll_user` (lines 183–232) requires ≥5 paths, loads with
    `torchaudio.load` (lines 200, 158), optionally calibrates a joint threshold,
    returns `{p_kw, p_spk, threshold}`.
- `src/demo.py`:
  - Hardcodes ALSA `hw:2,0` (`_ALSA_DMIC`, line 13) and `arecord` (lines 199–202);
    `_alsa_record` shells out to `arecord` (lines 36–50).
  - Single-profile only: loads one `p_kw`/`p_spk` from `enrollment.pt`
    (lines 331–343).
  - Hardcoded gates `sim_kw >= 0.60`, `sim_spk >= 0.50` (lines 130–132).
  - Overrides the checkpoint's scorer weights in `_load_model` to 0.30/0.65 by
    writing raw softplus inverses (lines 238–242).
- `src/config.py`: `SAMPLE_RATE=16000`, `N_MELS=80`, `WIN_LENGTH=400`,
  `HOP_LENGTH=160`, `MAX_AUDIO_SEC=2.0`, `MAX_FRAMES=200`, `EMBED_DIM=192`,
  `SCORE_W_KW=0.30`, `SCORE_W_SPK=0.65`, `DEFAULT_THRESHOLD=0.2222` (lines 7–12,
  17, 62–65).
- `src/eval/benchmark.py` evaluates with **unconditioned** embeddings, confirming
  the intended multi-profile path (previously read; checkpoint summary).
- Tests: `pytest.ini` takes precedence over `pyproject.toml`; `pythonpath=src`,
  `testpaths=tests`. Baseline `pytest -q` → **60 passed in 3.21 s**.
- `.gitignore` ignores `recordings/`, `*.wav`, `*.flac`, `*.pt`, `*.onnx`
  (lines 151–165). Demo assets must be force-added or whitelisted.

**Evidence:** file:line references above; `pytest -q` output.
**Not verified:** None for the file contents.
**Contradictions/resolution:** None.
**Other discoveries:** `src/demo.py` resets EMA and smooths but computes a
single-user joint score; it cannot rank multiple people. The new service should
not depend on `RealTimeDetector`.

## 5. Keyword matching (DISENT phonetic head)

### 2026-10-20 — Isolated-word cross-speaker test
**Subagent question:** Can one speaker's keyword prototype recognize the same
word spoken by other speakers, and reject different words?
**Answer:** Prototype `p_kw` from `yes_1353.wav` (unconditioned DISENT), scored
against 9 unique LibriPhrase clips (after md5 deduplication):
- `yes` other speakers: **0.5298, 0.5786, 0.6063, 0.6937** (min 0.5298, mean
  0.6817 including the prototype's 1.0)
- `no` clips: **0.2586, 0.3201, 0.3399, 0.3614** (max **0.3614**, mean 0.3200)
- **Gap min(yes) − max(no) = 0.1684.**
A threshold of **0.45** on cosine similarity separates these clips; a top1−top2
margin of **0.05–0.08** is reasonable.
**Evidence:** `research/artifacts/eval_matching.py` (first run),
`eval_librispeech.py` (deduped run), `eval_librispeech_results.json`
(`kw_isolated`).
**Not verified:** Other vocabulary words, noisy audio, whispered speech, accents
outside LibriSpeech.
**Contradictions/resolution:** The first run's `yes_min=0.5298` included duplicate
files; deduplication leaves the same minimum and removes three exact copies.
Results are unchanged for the cross-speaker claim.
**Other discoveries:** A 3-clip prototype that was mostly duplicates gave a
misleadingly high `yes_min=0.6304`; do not use duplicate-augmented prototypes as
evidence.

### 2026-10-21 — Keyword in continuous sentences: NEGATIVE result
**Subagent question:** Can the keyword be detected inside arbitrary sentences
(e.g. a wake word at the start of a command)?
**Answer:** **No, not reliably.** Using the isolated `yes` prototype and sliding
1.0 s windows (0.25 s hop) over 10 LibriSpeech sentences containing "yes" and 20
sentences without "yes":
- yes sentences: max-window score min **0.5484**, mean **0.6400**
- non-yes sentences: max-window score max **0.7352**, mean **0.5670**
- Full 2 s utterance: yes mean **0.4917**, non-yes mean **0.3991**
The distributions **overlap**; a max-over-windows rule produces false positives.
**Evidence:** `research/artifacts/eval_librispeech.py`,
`eval_librispeech_results.json` (`kw_sentences`); per-sentence rows in the JSON.
**Not verified:** A dedicated wake-word model (not in this repo); in-sentence
detection with a keyword-specific classifier head.
**Contradictions/resolution:** This overrides any assumption that the DISENT
phonetic embedding can act as a continuous wake-word detector. **Locked scope:**
the demo requires the keyword to be spoken as a short isolated utterance
(≤2 s, ideally 0.5–1.5 s), exactly as enrolled.
**Other discoveries:** Max-over-windows inflates false positives; score a single
energy-selected 2 s window instead of taking a max over many windows.

## 6. VAD, segmentation, and streaming behavior

### 2026-10-21 — Silero VAD API and measured behavior
**Subagent question:** Does Silero VAD work in 512-sample streaming mode, and
what are its timing/false-positive characteristics?
**Answer:**
- `from silero_vad import load_silero_vad, VADIterator`;
  `load_silero_vad(onnx=False, sampling_rate=16000, max_frames=512)` loads the
  bundled JIT model in **42 ms**.
- `VADIterator(model, threshold=0.5, sampling_rate=16000,
  min_silence_duration_ms=300, speech_pad_ms=100)`.
- At 16 kHz, each call **must** receive exactly **512 samples**; otherwise
  `ValueError` (source lines 112–115).
- Returns `{'start': sample}` when speech begins and `{'end': sample}` after
  `min_silence_duration_ms` of non-speech; may return `None`.
- Streaming 8 real 2–6 s LibriSpeech clips: **11.7–91.1 ms per clip** total
  VAD time (25–30 ms typical). Start events fire; end events fire only when the
  clip contains trailing silence. A clip that ends mid-speech emits no `end`.
- 10 s digital silence → **0** transitions; 10 s low-level noise
  (`randn*0.01`) → **0** transitions.
- Plan must force-finalize at `max_utterance_sec` (e.g. 6 s) and call
  `vad.reset_states()` after a forced finalize, otherwise a new start may not
  fire.
**Evidence:** `research/artifacts/eval_vad_aug.py`; installed
`silero_vad/utils_vad.py` lines 591–680, 112–115; `model.py` lines 1–40.
**Not verified:** Far-field/echo/background-music false positives; browser mic
noise suppression disabled behavior.
**Contradictions/resolution:** None.
**Other discoveries:** `VADIterator` imports only torch in the plain install;
ONNX is optional. Do not install `silero-vad[audio]` (torchaudio<2.10 conflict).

### 2026-10-21 — Enrollment augmentation performance and `trim_silence` bug
**Subagent question:** Can the existing enrollment augmentation run in a live
web wizard?
**Answer:** **No.** `augment_enrollment(5 clips, n_total=30)` took **141,987 ms
(142 s)** on this CPU; all 30 variants were unique, but the cost is dominated by
`torchaudio.functional.pitch_shift` (phase vocoder). A fast replacement using
only `F.interpolate` time-stretch + gain produced **15 variants in 70.4 ms**
(5 → 15), all unique. Use fast augmentation (or raw takes) in the web flow.
- `trim_silence` crashes for waveforms shorter than 1600 samples:
  `RuntimeError: maximum size for tensor at dimension 1 is 400 but size is 1600`
  (n=400, n=1000); works at n=1600+.
**Evidence:** `research/artifacts/eval_vad_aug.py`; fast-augmentation timing
script output.
**Not verified:** Whether 5 raw takes alone are enough for keyword matching
(only cross-speaker 1-clip data exists); fast-augmented variants' effect on
keyword accuracy (not measured).
**Contradictions/resolution:** The existing `enroll_user` path is correct but
unusable interactively. Keep it for CLI/back-compat; add a fast path for the app.
**Other discoveries:** `augment_enrollment` returns variable-length waveforms
(77,040–93,680 samples) because pitch shift changes duration; `LFBETransform`
pads/truncates, so batching is safe. Add a short-clip guard to `trim_silence` or
wrap it.

## 7. Browser audio and UI constraints

### 2026-10-20 — getUserMedia and AudioContext facts
**Subagent question:** What is required to capture microphone audio in the
browser, and how should 16 kHz audio be produced?
**Answer:**
- `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia({audio: ...})` is available only in a
  **secure context**; `navigator.mediaDevices` is `undefined` over plain HTTP on
  a non-localhost origin. Secure contexts include **HTTPS**, `file://`, and
  **`http://localhost`**. On failure: `NotAllowedError` (permission/insecure) or
  `NotFoundError` (no device).
  Source: <https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia>
- `new AudioContext({sampleRate})` accepts a requested sample rate; if
  unsupported it throws `NotSupportedError`. The default is the output device
  rate (often 44.1/48 kHz). Do **not** assume 16 kHz.
  Source: <https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/AudioContext>
- `AudioWorkletProcessor.process(inputs, outputs, parameters)` is called once per
  **128-frame** render quantum; each input channel is a `Float32Array` of 128
  samples (the spec may change, so read `channel.length`).
  Source: <https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioWorkletProcessor/process>
- `process()` must return `true` to keep the node alive.
- Chosen approach: request `AudioContext({sampleRate:16000})`; if it throws,
  create a native-rate context, declare `sample_rate` in the WS hello, and
  resample server-side with `torchaudio.functional.resample`. Send binary
  `Float32Array` batches (~2048–4096 samples) over WebSocket.
- For TTS, use `speechSynthesis.speak(new SpeechSynthesisUtterance(...))` in the
  browser (no server TTS dependency). Start listening from a user gesture so
  audio/TTS permissions are unlocked.
**Evidence:** MDN pages above; prior FastAPI WS echo test.
**Not verified:** `AudioContext({sampleRate:16000})` support on the user's exact
browser; real mic capture on this host (no browser, no sound card); Safari
behavior; `speechSynthesis` voice availability.
**Contradictions/resolution:** Do not rely on `MediaRecorder` + server FFmpeg
decode (torchcodec/FFmpeg is broken, and MediaRecorder codecs vary). Use Web
Audio + WAV encoding for enrollment.
**Other discoveries:** `enumerateDevices()` requires permission before labels are
available; include a device picker that refreshes after `getUserMedia`.

### 2026-10-20 — No microphone / no browser on the host
**Subagent question:** Can the demo run entirely on this machine?
**Answer:** No. There is **no sound card** (`arecord -l` → no soundcards) and
**no browser binary**. The server can run here, but the mic and UI must come from

-subj "/CN=disent-kws"` and run uvicorn with `--ssl-keyfile key.pem
--ssl-certfile cert.pem`.

local port forwarding.** Run the server bound to `127.0.0.1:8000` on this host,

`http://localhost:8000`. The browser sees **localhost**, which is a secure
context, so `getUserMedia` works; audio travels through the SSH tunnel. This is
the lowest-risk path given the host has no browser/mic. **UNVERIFIED** on the
user's actual SSH topology; fall back to upload/replay if port forwarding is not
available.

### 2026-10-21 — Silero import changes PyTorch thread count
**Subagent question:** Does importing Silero VAD affect DISENT inference speed?
**Answer:** Yes. `silero_vad/model.py` calls `torch.set_num_threads(1)` at import
time. After importing Silero, DISENT batch-1 latency on `(1,80,200)` was

The app must call `torch.set_num_threads(max(1, min(4, os.cpu_count() or 1)))`
after all imports, before serving. VAD itself is unaffected.
**Evidence:** timing script output above; `silero_vad/model.py` line 3.
**Not verified:** Optimal thread count on the demo machine under concurrent
WebSocket/uvicorn load.
**Contradictions/resolution:** None.
**Other discoveries:** Import order matters: import Silero first, then set
threads, then load DISENT/ECAPA. Re-check latency in `/api/health`.

## 11. Correction (2026-10-21): short-keyword speaker embeddings were zero-padded

The first end-to-end multi-person demo exposed a speaker-ID bug: Bob's 0.56 s
`stop` clip matched Alice (`-0.018` vs Bob's centroid) even though the same
speaker's 3–4 s enrollment sentences were clean (`0.75` mean self-similarity).

Root cause: the speaker path called `select_window(wav, 6.0)` for both
enrollment and scoring. That zero-pads every short clip to 6 s. ECAPA's
embedding drifts as the speech/silence ratio changes. Measured on Bob's clip
against a Bob centroid built without fixed padding:

| Input duration | Bob stop similarity |
|:---:|:---:|
| 0.56 s, unpadded | **+0.411** |
| 0.75 s | +0.305 |
| 1.00 s | +0.261 |
| 2.00 s | +0.225 |
| 3.00 s | +0.083 |
| 6.00 s | **−0.068** |

Batching the short clip with a 3 s clip also dropped it to `−0.033` because the
batch was padded to the longest item.

Fix: `app/audio.cap_window()` selects an energy window without padding.
`select_window()` remains the padded variant only for DISENT's fixed-length mel
input. `Engine.embed_speaker_batch()` encodes clips separately when lengths
differ by more than 1.25×, and `score_speakers()` / `speaker_centroid()` use
natural-length clips. Profiles were re-seeded after the fix.

Post-fix end-to-end (real server, CPU):

| Clip | Event | Person | Keyword | Speaker |
|:---|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|
| `bob_stop.wav` | `keyword` | Bob | 0.891 | 0.415 |
| `alice_stop.wav` | `keyword` | Alice | 0.851 | 0.305 |
| `unknown_stop.wav` | `keyword_unknown_speaker` | — | 0.921 | 0.145 |
| `alice_speech.wav` | `speech` | Alice | — | 0.858 |
| `bob_sentence_1.wav` | `speech` | Bob | — | 0.863 |
| `unknown_sentence.wav` | `unrecognized` | — | — | 0.248 max |

The WebSocket VAD path trims edges, so streamed scores are slightly lower than
offline (`Bob stop` 0.347 vs 0.415) but remain well above the 0.20 short-clip
threshold. Regression test:
`tests/test_app_inference.py::test_real_short_keyword_is_not_zero_padded`.
